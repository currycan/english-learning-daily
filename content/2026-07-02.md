In the past, people need the night sky to live.

In Australia, the Milky Way lights up the night. Aboriginal people watch stars to know when to pick food. Polynesians travel the Pacific Ocean by watching the stars. European sailors also use the sky to find their way. Ancient places like Stonehenge and Newgrange help people know time. People hold ceremonies during the shortest days of the year.

Today, we use GPS instead of the stars. But too much light at night makes it hard for people and animals, like birds, to see the night sky.

Difficult words: aboriginal (the first people who live in a country or area), sailor (a person who has a job on a ship or boat), ancient (very old).

## 🔗 表达 / Chunking Expressions

**the night sky** （夜空）
Refers to the sky as seen at night, often with stars, the moon, etc. Commonly used in general conversation and writing.
- We looked up at the clear night sky and saw so many stars.
- The city lights made it difficult to see the night sky.

**find their way** （找到方向；辨别方向）
To navigate or know the direction to go. Used for both literal and metaphorical directions.
- He used a map to find his way through the forest.
- She was lost, but a kind stranger helped her find her way to the hotel.

**know time** （知道时间；判断时间）
To understand or determine the time of day or year. Can be literal (telling time) or more general (understanding seasons or historical periods).
- The sundial helped ancient people know time.
- We need to know time to plan our meeting.

**makes it hard** （使之困难；使难以）
Causes something to become difficult or challenging. Often followed by 'for someone' or 'to do something'.
- The rain makes it hard to drive.
- His explanation makes it hard for me to understand the problem.
