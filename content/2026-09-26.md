The doorway effect is a common problem with your memory. You walk into a room and forget why you are there.

This happens because your brain thinks that a new event is starting when you walk through a door. To get ready for new things, your brain clears your memory. Scientists test this in real and virtual rooms. People forget more when they go through doors or do many things at once. Changing computer tabs can do the same thing.

If you forget your goal, you can walk back to the first room or say your idea again out loud.

Difficult words: common (usual), forget (not to remember something), goal (what you plan to do or get).

## 🔗 表达 / Chunking Expressions

**walk into a room** （走进一个房间）
Used to describe the action of entering a physical space. Common and straightforward.
- I walk into a room and suddenly can't remember what I needed.
- She walked into a room filled with flowers.

**forget why** （忘记为什么）
Used to express the forgetting of the reason or purpose for an action or intention. Frequently used in everyday conversation.
- I often forget why I opened the refrigerator.
- He walked away and then forgot why he was standing there.

**get ready for** （为...做好准备）
To prepare yourself or something for a particular activity or event. Used to indicate preparation.
- We need to get ready for the important meeting.
- The children were excited to get ready for their trip.

**clear your memory** （清除你的记忆）
This is a metaphorical expression describing the brain's process of erasing or making space in short-term memory. It's a simplified explanation of a cognitive process.
- Sometimes, after a long day, it feels like my brain tries to clear my memory of small details.
- The new software is designed to clear your memory of temporary files.
