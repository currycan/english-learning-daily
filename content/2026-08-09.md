A New Year’s resolution is a promise which you make to yourself. You say that you will start doing something good or stop doing something bad on 1 January.

Many people have problems with New Year’s resolutions. They often fail. Professor Scot Lear says that our goals must be SMART – Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Timely. An example is exercising three times a week for 30 minutes for three months. If there are problems, it is okay. Things are not always as we want.

Scot says that we must be kind to ourselves, learn from our mistakes, and not stop.

Difficult words: specific (clear), measurable (possible to measure), achievable (possible to do), relevant (useful, related to you).

## 🔗 表达 / Chunking Expressions

**New Year’s resolution** （新年决心）
A promise or decision made at the beginning of a new year to change a habit or start a new one. Used in informal and general contexts.
- My New Year's resolution is to read more books.
- She made a New Year's resolution to eat healthier.

**make to yourself** （对自己做出）
To decide to do something for your own benefit or commitment. Used in personal contexts.
- He made a promise to himself to get fit.
- I make a promise to myself every year to be more patient.

**learn from our mistakes** （从错误中学习）
To gain knowledge or understanding from errors or bad experiences. Used in general advice and self-improvement contexts.
- The best way to improve is to learn from our mistakes.
- Companies should learn from their mistakes to avoid future problems.
