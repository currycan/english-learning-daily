Five days after two catastrophic earthquakes struck Venezuela’s northern coast near Caracas, rescue teams are desperately searching the rubble for survivors.

While more than 1,700 people are confirmed dead, the final death toll is expected to rise significantly because approximately 46,000 people remain unaccounted for.

In coastal towns like Caraballeda, volunteers use specialized radar equipment and their bare hands to locate signs of life beneath collapsed buildings. Despite fading hopes, miraculous rescues keep families motivated. A mother and her baby were recently saved after being trapped for days; she kept her son alive by checking his breathing whenever he cried. However, at many disaster sites, official counting has paused.

Critics openly accuse the Venezuelan government of intentionally delaying the publication of accurate casualty numbers and point out a long history of systematically hiding bad news from the public. At the same time, thousands of families continue to wait in fear of bad news.

## 🔗 表达 / Chunking Expressions

**struck Venezuela’s northern coast** （袭击了委内瑞拉北部海岸）
This phrase describes a natural disaster, like an earthquake or storm, happening in a specific geographical area. It's often used in news reports about such events.
- The hurricane struck Florida’s southern coast with great force.
- An unexpected blizzard struck the northern coast, causing widespread disruption.

**searching the rubble** （搜寻瓦砾）
This is a common phrase used in the context of disasters where buildings have collapsed. It means to look carefully through the broken pieces of the destroyed structures for survivors or important items.
- Rescue workers spent days searching the rubble for any signs of life.
- The team was exhausted from searching the rubble after the explosion.

**remain unaccounted for** （下落不明）
This means that a number of people are missing and their location or status is unknown, usually after an accident or disaster.
- After the flood, hundreds of people remained unaccounted for.
- Investigators are concerned that several witnesses may still remain unaccounted for.

**point out a long history** （指出悠久的（历史））
This phrase is used to draw attention to a pattern of behavior or events that has occurred over a considerable period. It suggests that the current situation is not an isolated incident but part of a recurring trend.
- Historians point out a long history of similar conflicts in the region.
- The report points out a long history of environmental neglect in the area.
