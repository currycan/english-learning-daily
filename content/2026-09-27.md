The doorway effect is a common problem with your memory. You walk into a room and forget why you are there.

This happens because your brain thinks that a new event is starting when you walk through a door. To get ready for new things, your brain clears your memory. Scientists test this in real and virtual rooms. People forget more when they go through doors or do many things at once. Changing computer tabs can do the same thing.

If you forget your goal, you can walk back to the first room or say your idea again out loud.

Difficult words: common (usual), forget (not to remember something), goal (what you plan to do or get).

## 🔗 表达 / Chunking Expressions

**walk into a room** （走进一个房间）
Describes the action of entering a room. Common and straightforward.
- She walked into a room and immediately felt a sense of calm.
- He walked into a room filled with strangers and felt a little nervous.

**forget why you are there** （忘记你为什么在那里）
A specific instance of forgetting, often related to a loss of purpose or intention upon entering a new space.
- I often walk into a room and forget why I am there, especially after a long day.
- He paused in the doorway, admitting he had walked into a room and forgotten why he was there.

**clear your memory** （清除你的记忆）
Refers to the brain's process of removing or overwriting previous information to make space for new information. Can be literal or metaphorical.
- When you go through a doorway, your brain might clear your memory to prepare for new experiences.
- Sometimes, a good night's sleep can help clear your memory of stress from the day.

**at once** （立刻，一次）
Means immediately or simultaneously. It can refer to doing things at the same time or doing something very quickly.
- If you try to do too many things at once, you might get confused.
- The music stopped, and the audience applauded at once.

**Q: What is the doorway effect?**

**Q: According to the article, why does the doorway effect happen?**
**A (EN):** It happens because the brain perceives entering a new space (like through a door) as a new event starting, and to prepare for new things, it clears your memory.
**A (中):** 发生这种情况是因为大脑认为进入一个新空间（如穿过一扇门）是一个新事件的开始，为了准备新事物，它会清除你的记忆。

**Q: The article mentions that changing computer tabs can have a similar effect to walking through a door. What does this suggest about the nature of the doorway effect?**
**A (EN):** This suggests that the doorway effect is not solely about physical movement through a doorway, but rather about the cognitive transition and change of context that a new space or task represents. The brain treats these transitions as resets, impacting memory.
**A (中):** 这表明门口效应不仅仅与穿过门框的物理移动有关，更与新空间或任务所代表的认知转变和情境变化有关。大脑将这些转变视为重置，从而影响记忆。
